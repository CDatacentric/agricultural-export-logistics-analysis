# Findings and Recommendations

## Overview

The analysis produced a large number of observations across products, countries, suppliers, financial performance, logistics, delivery performance, and customer satisfaction. However, not every observation needs to become a business finding.

For the final report, I focused on the findings that were most relevant to management, supported by the available data, and capable of leading to a practical business action or further investigation.

The recommendations therefore do not assume that every unusual result represents a confirmed business problem. Where the available data identifies an area of concern but does not explain the underlying cause, the recommendation is to investigate further rather than make an unsupported conclusion.

---

# Finding 1 — The Business Is Profitable, but Performance Declined in 2025

The overall business generated approximately **$1.22 billion in Revenue** and **$360.8 million in Net Profit**, producing an overall Profit Margin of approximately **20%**.

At first glance, these results indicate a strong and profitable business. However, the year-over-year analysis revealed that **2025 recorded lower Revenue and Net Profit than 2024**.

This creates an important distinction between the company's current financial position and its performance direction. The business remained profitable, but the decline in 2025 suggests that management should not rely only on the strength of the overall cumulative figures.

### Recommendation

Management should investigate the drivers behind the 2025 decline, particularly the products, markets, suppliers, and periods contributing most to the reduction in Revenue and Net Profit.

The investigation should begin with the weaker quarters identified in the analysis and then drill down into product and country performance to determine whether the decline was caused by changes in sales volume, product mix, pricing, market activity, or other operational factors.

---

# Finding 2 — Revenue Performance Is Strongly Connected to Order Quantity

The product analysis demonstrated that high Revenue does not automatically mean that a product has the strongest underlying profitability.

Some high-Revenue results were strongly influenced by **Order Quantity**, meaning that sales volume was an important contributor to the Revenue generated by those products.

This is an important distinction because a product can generate substantial Revenue through high transaction volume without necessarily having the strongest Profit Margin.

### Recommendation

Management should evaluate product performance using **Revenue, Order Quantity, Net Profit, and Profit Margin together** rather than ranking products using Revenue alone.

Products generating high volumes but relatively weaker margins may require further investigation into pricing, discounts, production costs, or other cost components.

At the same time, products generating both strong volume and strong profitability could provide useful opportunities for growth or increased market focus.

---

# Finding 3 — Financial Performance Is Concentrated Across Markets

Country-level analysis showed that business performance was not evenly distributed across all markets.

The **United Kingdom** was one of the strongest contributors, generating approximately **$136.1 million in Revenue** and **$39.5 million in Net Profit**.

This demonstrates the importance of market-level analysis because the overall company result can conceal substantial differences in contribution between countries.

### Recommendation

Management should investigate the characteristics behind stronger-performing markets and determine whether successful patterns can be replicated in lower-performing markets.

This could include examining product mix, order volume, pricing, supplier relationships, shipping conditions, and customer demand by market.

The objective should not simply be to increase investment in the strongest market, but to understand **why** it performs strongly and whether those factors can inform decisions across the wider business.

---

# Finding 4 — The 2025 Decline Becomes More Relevant When Viewed by Quarter

The annual comparison showed that 2025 performed below 2024, but the quarterly analysis provided additional context by showing that the weaker performance became more apparent during the later part of the year.

This demonstrates why annual reporting alone can be insufficient for management decision-making. An annual figure can identify that performance changed, while quarterly analysis helps identify **when** the change became more significant.

### Recommendation

Management should use quarterly reporting as an early-warning mechanism rather than waiting until the end of a financial year to identify deteriorating performance.

When a quarter shows a significant change, the analysis should be drilled down by Product, Country, Supplier, and Order activity to identify the specific areas contributing to the movement.

---

# Finding 5 — An Unusual December 2025 Result Requires Validation

The monthly analysis identified an unusually weak performance in **December 2025**.

Although this is an important observation, I would not classify the result as a confirmed business problem based on the available data alone. A sharp monthly movement could represent an actual change in business activity, seasonality, a reporting issue, or another factor that is not captured in the dataset.

This distinction is important because an unusual value should trigger investigation rather than automatically become a business conclusion.

### Recommendation

Management should validate the underlying December 2025 transactions and compare the period against historical December performance and surrounding months.

If the decline is confirmed as a genuine business event, further analysis should determine whether it was associated with specific products, countries, suppliers, order volumes, or operational conditions.

---

# Finding 6 — Shipping Method Alone Does Not Explain Shipping Cost

During data preparation, I initially investigated whether Shipping Method could be used to estimate the 152 missing Shipping Cost values.

I compared Air, Road, and Sea shipments and examined Quantity alongside Shipping Cost. The investigation showed that Shipping Cost varied considerably within the same Shipping Method and that the available data did not provide a consistent enough relationship for Shipping Method alone to serve as a reliable estimator.

This finding influenced the data-cleaning methodology and also provided an important analytical lesson: an apparently logical business variable should not automatically be treated as a reliable predictor without testing it against the available data.

### Recommendation

Management should avoid evaluating shipping methods using Shipping Cost alone.

A more complete logistics analysis should consider Shipping Cost together with Quantity, Processing Time, Delivery Delay, shipment characteristics, and customer outcomes.

If logistics optimization becomes a priority, additional variables such as shipment weight, distance, freight contracts, fuel surcharges, and insurance should be captured because these could provide stronger explanations for cost differences.

---

# Finding 7 — Operational Performance Should Be Viewed Alongside Customer Experience

The dashboard included Average Delivery Delay and Average Satisfaction Score alongside the financial KPIs.

This was intentional because strong financial performance does not necessarily mean that operational and customer outcomes are equally strong.

The analysis showed that customer satisfaction can be monitored at a high level, but the available dataset does not contain enough detailed customer feedback to reliably explain the causes behind satisfaction differences.

### Recommendation

Management should continue monitoring customer satisfaction alongside delivery performance and other operational measures.

To make future analysis more actionable, the business should consider capturing additional customer feedback variables such as:

- Reason for dissatisfaction
- Complaint category
- Delivery-related complaints
- Product-related complaints
- Service-related complaints
- Customer feedback comments

This would allow future analysis to move from identifying **where satisfaction is lower** to understanding **why satisfaction is lower**.

---

# Finding 8 — Supplier Performance Warrants Further Investigation

Supplier-level analysis showed differences in financial contribution across suppliers.

However, the available dataset does not contain enough information to determine whether a supplier generating lower Revenue or Net Profit is necessarily underperforming.

Supplier performance may be affected by factors such as product mix, order volume, pricing arrangements, or market allocation.

### Recommendation

Management should evaluate supplier performance using a broader supplier scorecard that combines financial and operational measures.

Future analysis could include:

- Purchase or production cost
- Product quality
- Delivery reliability
- Order fulfillment
- Supplier pricing
- Defect or return rates
- Contract terms

This would allow supplier decisions to be based on a broader measure of performance rather than Revenue or Profit alone.

---

# Priority Recommendations

Although several recommendations emerged from the analysis, I would prioritize the following actions:

### 1. Investigate the 2025 Financial Decline

Break down the decline by quarter, product, country, supplier, and order volume to identify the primary contributors.

### 2. Monitor Product Profitability Alongside Sales Volume

Evaluate Revenue, Quantity, Net Profit, and Profit Margin together to distinguish high-volume products from genuinely high-performing products.

### 3. Investigate Strong and Weak Markets

Use the strongest markets as a starting point for understanding successful product, pricing, and demand patterns that may be transferable to other markets.

### 4. Strengthen Logistics Data Collection

Capture additional shipping variables such as weight, distance, and freight-related costs to support more accurate logistics analysis.

### 5. Improve Customer Feedback Data

Collect structured reasons behind customer satisfaction and dissatisfaction so future analysis can identify the causes of customer-experience problems.

# Final Business Perspective

The most important outcome of this analysis was not a single KPI or chart. It was the ability to move from an overall business result into increasingly specific questions.

The business was profitable overall, but the year-over-year analysis revealed a decline. Product analysis showed that Revenue could be strongly influenced by Order Quantity. Country analysis showed that financial contribution was uneven across markets. Quarterly and monthly analysis identified periods requiring further investigation. The logistics investigation demonstrated that an intuitive assumption about Shipping Method did not hold strongly enough in the available data.

These findings demonstrate why business analysis should not stop at reporting the first result produced by a dataset.

The dashboard provides management with the **what**. The PivotTable exploration provides more context around **where** the patterns occur. The recommendations identify **what should be investigated or considered next**.

Where the dataset does not provide enough evidence to establish a cause, the appropriate conclusion is not to speculate. It is to identify the information that would be required to answer the question more reliably.

That distinction is an important part of evidence-based decision-making.

---

# Key Takeaway

The project demonstrates a complete analytical process in which data preparation, exploratory analysis, dashboard development, and business recommendations were connected rather than treated as separate tasks.

The final recommendations are therefore based not only on what the dashboard displays, but on the deeper investigation performed throughout the project.
